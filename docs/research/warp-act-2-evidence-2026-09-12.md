The research distinction discussed with the user is between the effect of the rewrite method and the mechanism behind the browser agent's behavior. The accepted matched study gives the rewriting model TP diagnosis or ordinary critique, with one rewrite opportunity per arm. It measures whether that change improves attack outcomes. A stronger claim would attribute a behavior change to the browser agent's internal belief that it is being evaluated. A cue can also directly prompt caution, and a rewrite can change how persuasive the attack is. Changes in TP or probe scores alone do not separate those paths.

A mechanism study would need additional evidence about what its awareness measure and intervention change: held-out measurement validation, controls for safety wording and task ability, comparable task/starting-state/exposure conditions, and declared assumptions with sensitivity analysis. Activation steering is one possible intervention on a model with accessible internal activations; matched control directions and utility checks would still be needed. This is a proposed research direction, not an accepted new experiment or a sufficient checklist for proof. The general distinction between an intervention's effect and its mechanism follows [Imai, Keele and Tingley, A General Approach to Causal Mediation Analysis](https://imai.fas.harvard.edu/research/files/BaronKenny.pdf). The existing WARP execution plan supplies the project-specific scope.
